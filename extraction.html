Clear, simple aftercare for tooth and wisdom-tooth extractions — everything you need to avoid dry socket and recover comfortably.

First 48 hours Recovery timeline

It all comes down to the clot

After any extraction, a blood clot forms in the socket — nature's bandage over the exposed bone and nerves. Protecting it is the single most important thing you can do.

Why the clot matters

The clot shields the bone and nerve endings while new tissue heals underneath. Nearly every aftercare rule below exists for one reason: to keep that clot safely in place.

What is dry socket?

If the clot is dislodged too early, the bone and nerves are left exposed — a highly painful condition called dry socket. Careful aftercare in the first few days is what prevents it.

Wisdom teeth ask for more patience. They often involve surgical bone removal or stitches, so recovery is stretched out and swelling is more intense. The same rules apply — just for longer.

The first 24–48 hours

These rules apply to every extraction to protect the clot and set up smooth healing.

  1. 1

    Control the bleeding

    First 1–2 hours. Keep firm pressure on the gauze pad by biting down continuously for 30–45 minutes. If bleeding continues, replace it with fresh, clean gauze and bite firmly for another 30 minutes.

  2. 2

    Protect the clot

    First 24 hours. Do not spit, brush next to the site, or use a straw. The suction from spitting or drinking through a straw can pull the clot right out of the socket. Swallow saliva normally, and if you must rinse, wait until day 2.

  3. 3

    Manage pain & swelling

    First 48 hours. Take prescribed pain medication before the local anaesthetic fully wears off. If you have swelling, apply an ice pack to the outside of the cheek — 20 minutes on, 20 minutes off — to help keep it down.

  4. 4

    Gentle hygiene & rinsing

    Starting day 2. Once 24 hours have passed, gently rinse with warm salt water (½ tsp salt in a cup) after meals to keep the area clean. Don't swish hard — let the water roll around your mouth and empty over the sink without forcibly spitting.

Routine vs. wisdom-tooth recovery

The rules are the same, but surgical and impacted wisdom teeth ask you to manage recovery more intensely. Here's how they compare.

Routine / normal tooth
Wisdom tooth (surgical / impacted)
Diet
Soft foods for 1–2 days. Avoid sharp foods (chips, nuts) that can poke the socket.
Soft foods for 3–7 days. Stick to liquids, smoothies (no straws), yoghurt, mashed potato and scrambled eggs until jaw mobility improves.
Swelling & bruising
Minimal to none.
Some swelling and facial bruising are common.
Physical activity
Rest on the day of surgery; resume light activity the next day.
Take it easy for 2–3 days. Avoid heavy lifting or intense cardio — a raised heart rate can make the socket throb and bleed again.
Stitches / sutures
Rarely needed unless several adjacent teeth are removed.
Frequently used. May be dissolvable (gone in 7–10 days) or removed by the dentist at a follow-up.

Eating after extraction

Soft, cool, no-straw foods protect the clot while you heal. Chew on the opposite side and ease back to normal as comfort allows.

Enjoy

  • Yoghurt & smoothies (by spoon, no straw)
  • Mashed potato & scrambled eggs
  • Lukewarm soup & broth
  • Oatmeal & porridge
  • Congee (rice porridge)
  • Soft, well-cooked pasta
  • Applesauce & ripe banana

Avoid

  • Straws — suction can pull the clot out
  • Chips, nuts, popcorn & seeds
  • Crunchy or sharp foods near the socket
  • Very hot food & drinks (first 24h)
  • Chewy or sticky foods
  • Alcohol & smoking

💡 Tip: Skip the straw for at least a week — the suction is one of the most common causes of a dislodged clot and dry socket.

Recovery timeline

Everyone heals at their own pace — and wisdom-tooth surgery takes longer — but here's the typical journey.

  1. 1
    Day 1

    Clot forms

    A protective clot forms in the socket. Bite on gauze to control bleeding, rest, and leave the area completely alone.

  2. 2
    Day 2

    Start gentle care

    Begin warm salt-water rinses after meals. Keep protecting the clot — still no straws, spitting, or brushing right on the site.

  3. 3
    Days 2–3

    Swelling peaks

    For wisdom teeth, swelling and bruising are usually at their worst. Switch from ice to warm compresses after 48 hours to ease stiff jaw muscles.

  4. 4
    Days 3–4

    Dry-socket watch

    This is when dry socket is most likely. Sudden, throbbing pain that spreads toward your ear and isn't eased by medication? Call us.

  5. 5
    Week 1

    Soft tissue heals

    The gum starts closing over the socket and any dissolvable stitches begin to disappear. Most routine extractions feel much better by now.

  6. 6
    Weeks 2–4

    Socket fills in

    New gum tissue fills the socket. You can steadily ease back toward your normal diet and routine.

  7. 7
    Months 1–3

    Bone remodels

    Beneath the gum, the bone gradually remodels and smooths over as the site fully matures.

Timelines vary. Surgical and impacted wisdom-tooth extractions take longer to settle than a simple tooth. Always follow the specific instructions from your dentist for your procedure.

Warning signs — when to call us

Contact the clinic promptly if you notice any of these — they can point to a dry socket or an infection.

Severe throbbing pain

Intense pain radiating to the ear that isn't relieved by pain medication, usually starting 3–4 days after surgery — a classic dry-socket sign.

Foul taste or odour

A bad taste or smell coming from the extraction site.

Fever or worsening swelling

A high fever, or swelling that gets worse after the third day — possible signs of infection.

Active bleeding

Bright-red bleeding that doesn't slow down after applying steady pressure with gauze.

Lasting numbness

Numbness or tingling in the lip, chin, or tongue that doesn't fade long after the anaesthetic should have worn off.

Reaction to medication

Rash, itching, trouble breathing, or nausea after taking prescribed medicine. Severe reactions are an emergency.

Not sure? Call us.

For heavy bleeding, spreading swelling, high fever, or a severe reaction to medication, contact us right away — or seek urgent medical care.
