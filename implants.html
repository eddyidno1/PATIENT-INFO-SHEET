A simple, step-by-step guide to recovering comfortably after dental implant surgery — from your first 24 hours to a fully healed implant.

Day 1 care Healing timeline

Immediate care — Day 1

The first 24 hours protect the blood clot that begins healing. Keep it simple: rest, gentle pressure, and leave the area alone.

  1. 1

    Bite on the gauze

    Keep firm, steady pressure on the gauze for 30–60 minutes to control bleeding. Replace it if it soaks through. A little oozing for the first day is normal.

  2. 2

    Protect the clot

    For the first 24 hours: no rinsing, no spitting, no straws, and no smoking. Suction and swishing can dislodge the clot and delay healing.

  3. 3

    Leave the site alone

    Don't touch it with your tongue, finger, or toothbrush. Avoid poking at any stitches or the healing cap.

  4. 4

    Rest with your head up

    Take it easy for the rest of the day. Keep your head slightly elevated — even while sleeping — to reduce throbbing and swelling.

  5. 5

    Start your medication

    Take any prescribed pain relief or antibiotics exactly as directed — starting before the numbness fully wears off keeps you ahead of discomfort.

  6. 6

    Begin icing

    Apply an ice pack to the outside of your cheek early (see the next section for timing) to keep swelling down.

💡 Tip: Set your soft foods, medication, gauze, and an ice pack out before the anaesthetic wears off, so you can rest without getting up.

Managing comfort & swelling

Some swelling and tenderness are a normal part of healing. Here's how to stay ahead of it.

Ice, on and off

First 24–48 hours: ice the cheek 20 minutes on, 20 minutes off, while you're awake. After 48 hours, switch to gentle warmth.

Expect a peak

Swelling and bruising often peak on day 2–3, then steadily improve. That's normal — it doesn't mean anything is wrong.

Stay ahead of pain

Take pain relief on the schedule we gave you rather than waiting for it to hurt. Never exceed the directed dose.

Salt-water rinses

Starting day 2, gently rinse with warm salt water (½ tsp salt in a cup) after meals. Let it fall out of your mouth — don't swish hard.

Sleep elevated

Prop yourself up with an extra pillow for the first few nights to ease throbbing and reduce morning swelling.

Bruising is okay

Light bruising on the cheek or jaw can appear and may take a week or so to fade. It's a normal response to surgery.

💡 Normal vs. call us: Mild swelling that improves after day 3 is expected. Swelling that worsens after day 3, with fever or increasing pain, can signal infection — call the office.

Diet & oral hygiene

Nourish yourself with soft, cool foods and keep your mouth clean — gently — while the implant site heals.

Eating

  • Stick to cool, soft foods for the first 24–48 hours
  • Chew on the opposite side, away from the implant
  • Stay well hydrated with water
  • Ease back to firmer foods as comfort allows
  • No hot, spicy, crunchy, or seedy foods
  • No straws, smoking, or alcohol
  • Nothing hard or chewy near the site

Oral hygiene

  • Keep brushing your other teeth normally
  • From day 2, use gentle warm salt-water rinses after meals
  • Ease a soft brush near the site as it heals and feels comfortable
  • Use any prescribed medicated rinse exactly as directed
  • Don't brush directly on the surgical site on day 1
  • No vigorous rinsing or spitting for the first 24 hours
  • Don't disturb stitches or the healing cap

What to watch out for

Complications are uncommon, but it helps to know the signs. If you notice any of these, don't wait — contact us.

Heavy bleeding

Bleeding that won't slow with firm gauze pressure after an hour, or that suddenly gets heavier.

Worsening pain

Pain that increases after day 3 instead of easing, or that isn't controlled by your medication.

Spreading swelling or fever

Swelling that grows after day 3, warmth, a bad taste or pus, or a fever — possible signs of infection.

Lasting numbness

Numbness or tingling in the lip, chin, or tongue that doesn't fade as the anaesthetic wears off.

Loose implant or cap

The implant, healing cap, or a temporary feeling loose, or something small comes away — save it and call us.

Reaction to medication

Rash, itching, trouble breathing, or nausea after taking prescribed medicine. Severe reactions are an emergency.

Not sure? Call us.

For heavy bleeding, spreading swelling, high fever, or a severe reaction to medication, contact us right away — or seek urgent medical care.

Soft-food shopping list

Stock up before your surgery day so healing-friendly foods are ready at home. Here's a handy checklist.

Proteins

  • Eggs (soft-scrambled)
  • Greek yogurt
  • Cottage cheese
  • Silken tofu
  • Protein / meal-replacement shakes
  • Flaked soft fish

Dairy & alternatives

  • Milk or plant milk
  • Smooth pudding & custard
  • Ice cream / frozen yogurt
  • Soft, mild cheeses
  • Kefir or drinkable yogurt

Fruit & veg

  • Ripe bananas
  • Avocado
  • Applesauce
  • Mashed or steamed soft veg
  • Smoothie ingredients (no seeds)

Grains & carbs

  • Oatmeal / porridge
  • Mashed potato or sweet potato
  • Well-cooked pasta
  • Soft rice or risotto
  • Cream of wheat

Soups & drinks

  • Broths & blended soups (lukewarm)
  • Bottled water
  • Electrolyte drinks
  • Herbal tea (cooled)

Handy extras

  • Blender for smoothies & soups
  • Extra gauze & a reusable ice pack
  • Small spoons
  • Table salt (for rinses)
  • Soft toothbrush

💡 Skip: anything hot, spicy, crunchy, chewy, or with seeds, nuts, or small grains that can lodge in the site (popcorn, rice cakes, granola, chips).

Healing timeline & recovery stages

Every patient heals at their own pace, but here's the typical journey from surgery to your finished implant.

  1. 1
    Day 1

    Clot forms

    Bleeding settles and a protective clot forms. Rest, ice, and protect the site. Expect mild oozing and numbness wearing off.

  2. 2
    Days 2–3

    Peak swelling

    Swelling and bruising are usually at their most noticeable, then begin to improve. Start gentle salt-water rinses.

  3. 3
    Days 3–7

    Soft tissue heals

    Discomfort eases and the gum begins closing over the site. Any non-dissolving stitches are typically removed around a week.

  4. 4
    Weeks 1–2

    Back to routine

    Most people return to normal daily activities and gentler foods. You may have a follow-up visit so we can check healing.

  5. 5
    Weeks 2–6

    Gums mature

    The gum tissue firms up and settles. Keep the area clean and continue easing back toward your normal diet.

  6. 6
    Months 2–6

    Osseointegration

    The implant fuses with your jawbone — the key to a strong, lasting result. This happens quietly beneath the gum over several months.

  7. 7
    Final

    Your new tooth

    Once the implant is fully integrated, we place the abutment and your custom crown. Your new smile is complete.

Timelines vary. Healing speed depends on your health, the number of implants, bone grafting, and whether a temporary was placed. Your surgeon's specific plan always takes priority over these general stages.
