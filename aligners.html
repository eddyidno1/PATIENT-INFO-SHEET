Aligner care The journey

The clear aligner journey

A structured, carefully guided path from your first visit to a confident, aligned smile. Here's what to expect with clear aligners.

  1. 1
    Getting started

    Consultation & assessment

    Your clear aligner journey begins just like braces — with a thorough consultation and assessment. We examine your teeth, gums and bite, take any scans, photographs and X-rays we need, and talk through your goals to confirm that clear aligners are the right choice for you.

  2. 2
    About 2 weeks later

    Treatment plan & simulation approval

    Around two weeks after your consultation, your personalised plan is ready. Using a digital 3D scan of your teeth, we show you a simulation of how they'll move — step by step — from where they are today to the final result, and walk you through the planned movements and the expected treatment duration. Once you're happy with the plan, you approve it so your custom aligners can be manufactured.

  3. 3
    3–4 weeks later

    Start your treatment

    About three to four weeks later, your custom-made aligners are ready and treatment begins. At this visit we bond any small tooth-coloured attachments that help guide your teeth, fit your first set of aligners, and show you exactly how to insert, remove, wear and clean them — including how many hours a day to wear each set.

  4. 4
    Every 4–8 weeks

    Regular checks

    Throughout treatment you'll come in for regular progress checks, usually every four to eight weeks. We make sure your teeth are tracking to plan, hand over your next sets of aligners, and make any small refinements needed to keep everything on course.

  5. 5
    Aftercare

    Retention

    Once your teeth have reached their final position, we move into the retention phase. To keep your new smile stable and stop your teeth drifting back, we provide retainers and explain how and when to wear them for a lasting result.

Wearing & caring for your aligners

Clear aligners only work when you wear them — and stay invisible when you look after them. Here's how to wear, clean, and store them day to day.

Wear them 22 hours a day

Wearing your aligners at least 22 hours a day is the single most important factor in effective treatment. Only take them out to eat, to drink anything other than water, and to clean your teeth.

Cold water is fine

You don't need to remove your aligners to drink cold or room-temperature water. Avoid hot drinks with them in, though — heat warps the plastic.

Rinse every time

Each time you take your aligners out, rinse them with room-temperature water to wash away saliva and plaque before storing or reinserting.

Clean twice a day

Clean your aligners twice a day with a soft toothbrush and a little mild soap. Skip toothpaste — it's abrasive and dulls the plastic.

Deep clean if discoloured

Only if they look heavily discoloured, soak them in an aligner cleaning-tablet solution for about 15 minutes (optional). An ultrasonic cleaner also works well.

Brush before reinserting

When you can, brush and floss after every meal or snack before putting your aligners back in. If you can't brush right away, at least rinse your mouth and aligners with water first.

Use your chewies

Bite on your chewies every time you put a tray in, and again for a few minutes morning and night. This seats the aligners fully against your teeth and makes treatment more effective.

Store in the case

Always keep your aligners and chewies in their case when they're out — never wrap them in a napkin, where they're easily lost or thrown away.

A little soreness is normal

It's normal for a new set to feel tight or mildly sore for a day or two as your teeth move — this is part of the process and settles quickly.

Never use hot water. Heat warps the plastic and ruins the fit. Skip coloured mouthwash, denture cleaners not made for aligners, and abrasive toothpaste — they cause staining and cloudiness.

Handling emergencies

True orthodontic emergencies are rare. Most issues can wait until office hours — here's how to stay comfortable in the meantime.

Lost or broken aligner

Go back to your previous set to hold your progress, then call us. Don't skip ahead to the next set without checking first.

Losing track

If a gap forms between an aligner and your teeth, it's "losing track". Wear that aligner for longer each day, and use your chewies every time you reinsert it. If the gap is still there after 7 days of diligent wear, contact us.

Need help now?

Facial swelling, trauma, uncontrolled bleeding, or severe pain need prompt care — call us right away.

If an attachment comes off

Attachments are tiny tooth-coloured add-ons bonded to your teeth to help your aligners work. If one comes loose it's usually not an emergency — here's what to do.

What attachments do

Attachments are small tooth-coloured "bumps" bonded to some teeth, giving your aligners something to grip so they can move your teeth precisely.

If one comes off

They occasionally come off — most often in the first few days after bonding. Don't worry: keep wearing your aligners full-time as normal and contact us to arrange a quick re-bond — it's not urgent.
