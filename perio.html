Care for your gums
as they heal.

Gentle, step-by-step aftercare following deep cleaning or gum surgery — to ease sensitivity, prevent infection, and help your gums reattach to healthy roots.

First 24 hours Warning signs

Immediate care — the first 24 hours

The first day is about managing numbness, a little bleeding, and early discomfort while the gum tissue settles.

  1. 1

    Protect numb tissues

    First 2–4 hours. Don't eat or drink anything hot until the local anaesthetic has fully worn off — it's very easy to accidentally bite or burn your tongue, cheeks, and lips while they're numb.

  2. 2

    Manage bleeding

    First 12 hours. Slight bleeding, pink-tinged saliva, or minor oozing is normal on day one. If it seems persistent, place damp clean gauze — or a damp black tea bag — over the area and hold firm pressure for 20 minutes. Avoid forceful spitting or rinsing, which can disturb early clotting.

  3. 3

    Take pain relief early

    Before numbness fades. Take an over-the-counter anti-inflammatory (such as ibuprofen) before the numbness fully wears off. Perio treatment works deep in the gum tissue, so keeping inflammation down early makes a real difference.

💡 Tip: Rest for the remainder of the day and keep your head slightly elevated — it helps calm throbbing and keeps early bleeding to a minimum.

Diet & nutrition

Your gums will be tender for a few days. Soft, cool foods for the first 1–3 days protect the healing tissue from irritation and injury.

Enjoy · days 1–3

  • Scrambled eggs
  • Yoghurt & cottage cheese
  • Smoothies (by spoon or cup — no straws)
  • Mashed potato
  • Cooled soups & broths
  • Soft, nutrient-dense foods that need little chewing

Avoid

  • Chips, nuts, popcorn & seeds — they lodge in gum pockets
  • Very hot foods & drinks
  • Spicy foods that irritate raw tissue
  • Straws (suction disturbs healing)
  • Alcohol
  • Smoking
Skip alcohol and smoking for 48–72 hours. Both significantly slow tissue healing and raise the risk of complications — the longer you can avoid them, the better your gums recover.

Oral hygiene routine

Keeping clean is essential — but be gentle so you don't disturb the healing gumline. Here's how it changes over the first week.

Day 1 Day of treatment — leave it be
  • Don't brush or floss the treated areas
  • Don't use commercial mouthwashes with alcohol — they sting badly
  • You may gently brush and floss the untreated teeth
Days 2–7 Gentle transition
  • Warm salt-water rinses: ½ tsp salt in 8 oz warm water, 3–4× a day (especially after meals). Let it bathe the area and spit gently — don't swish hard.
  • Targeted brushing: use an extra-soft brush at a 45° angle to the gumline, with light circular motions. A little bleeding as tissue heals is normal.
  • Flossing: resume very gently — slide floss carefully between teeth, never snapping it down into tender gums.

Normal healing vs. warning signs

Knowing what's expected keeps you calm — and helps you spot the few signs that mean you should call us.

Normal healing
Tooth sensitivity
As tartar comes off, roots meet air, hot, and cold. Sensitivity usually peaks in the first week, then fades. A desensitizing toothpaste (e.g. Sensodyne) helps.
Gum recession / new gaps
As swelling subsides, gums shrink and fit tightly again — you may see small new spaces near the gumline. That's inflammation resolving and tissue getting healthy.
Mild soreness
The jaw and gums can feel bruised or tender for 2–4 days.
Call the clinic if…
  • Severe pain that isn't relieved by over-the-counter medication
  • Prolonged, heavy bleeding that doesn't stop with pressure
  • Increased swelling or a high fever a few days after treatment
  • A foul taste or pus discharging from the gumline

Keeping gums healthy for good

Periodontal disease is managed for life, not cured once. A steady routine keeps it from coming back.

Perio maintenance cleanings

Most perio patients switch to professional cleanings every 3–4 months (rather than every 6). This keeps deep pockets clear before bacteria can re-inflame the gums.

Daily deep cleaning

Brush twice daily and clean between every tooth with floss or interdental brushes — plaque left along the gumline is what restarts the disease.

Ease lingering sensitivity

Keep using a desensitizing toothpaste and a soft brush. Sensitivity from exposed roots usually settles over the weeks that follow.

Stop smoking

Smoking is one of the biggest drivers of gum disease and slows healing. Quitting is the single most powerful thing you can do for your gums.
