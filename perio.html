The first hours & pain relief

A deep clean (periodontal debridement) is important for managing gum disease. The first few hours are simply about letting the anaesthetic wear off and staying comfortable.

  1. 1

    Let the numbness wear off

    First 2–4 hours. If a local anaesthetic was used, wait until the numbness has fully worn off before eating — it's very easy to accidentally bite or burn your tongue, cheeks or lips while they're numb.

  2. 2

    Pain relief — only if you need it

    As needed. Discomfort is rarely a problem after a deep clean. If you do feel sore, paracetamol 500 mg — two tablets, up to four times a day — usually settles it. Follow the packet directions and check with us or your pharmacist if you take other medications.

  3. 3

    Expect some tenderness

    First few days. Mild gum tenderness, soreness, or a little bleeding is normal for a few days as your gums settle. Take it easy and avoid poking or prodding the treated area.

💡 Tip: Good daily cleaning (below) does more for your recovery than any painkiller — it's the key to healing and getting your gum disease under control.

Eating & drinking

Wait for any numbness to pass, then keep to a soft diet for the first 24 hours so your gums aren't irritated while they settle.

First 24 hours · soft diet

  • Scrambled eggs
  • Yoghurt & cottage cheese
  • Smoothies (by spoon or cup)
  • Mashed potato
  • Cooled soups & broths
  • Soft, nutrient-dense foods that need little chewing

Avoid

  • Eating while your mouth is still numb
  • Alcohol — it delays healing
  • Smoking — it delays healing
  • Very hot foods & drinks
  • Hard, crunchy foods near the treated gums
  • Spicy foods that can irritate tender tissue
Avoid alcohol and smoking while you heal. Both significantly slow tissue healing and raise the risk of complications — the longer you can avoid them, the better your gums recover.

Oral hygiene routine

This is the most important part. Consistent, thorough cleaning is essential for healing and for improving your gum disease — keep it up every day.

Twice a day, every day Brush & clean between your teeth
  • Clean between your teeth twice a day with interdental brushes (e.g. Piksters) or dental floss — the gumline between teeth is where gum disease takes hold, so this matters most.
  • Brush thoroughly twice a day with a manual or electric toothbrush, angling the bristles gently towards the gumline.
  • Be gentle around the treated gums — a little bleeding as you clean is normal at first and improves as they heal.
Twice daily · for 2 weeks Antiseptic mouthwash
  • Rinse with an antiseptic mouthwash (e.g. Curasept) twice a day for two weeks to support healing.
  • Use it after brushing, and follow the directions on the bottle.

What to expect vs. when to call

Knowing what's normal keeps you at ease — and helps you spot the few signs that mean you should get in touch.

What to expect
Tenderness & light bleeding
Mild gum tenderness, soreness, or a little bleeding can happen for a few days after treatment.
Tooth sensitivity
Teeth may feel sensitive, especially to cold or sweet foods. This can be brief or last a few weeks. A desensitising toothpaste (e.g. Sensodyne) helps.
Red or swollen gums
Your gums may look slightly red or swollen at first, then settle and firm up as they heal.
Call the clinic if…
  • Excessive or heavy bleeding
  • Increasing swelling
  • Pain that isn't settling with paracetamol
  • Any other unusual symptoms you're unsure about

Not sure? Call us.

Follow-up & long-term care

Gum disease is managed for life, not cured once. Coming back for reviews and keeping up your daily care is what keeps it under control.

Come back for your review

It's important to return for your review appointment so we can check how your gums are healing and plan any further care. We'll let you know the right timing for you.

Professional cleaning every 3–6 months

Long-term maintenance is essential. Depending on your gum condition, we recommend a professional clean every 3–6 months to keep deep pockets clear before bacteria can re-inflame the gums.

Clean well every day

Brush twice a day and clean between every tooth with floss or interdental brushes — plaque left along the gumline is what restarts the disease.

Stop smoking

Smoking is one of the biggest drivers of gum disease and slows healing. Quitting is the single most powerful thing you can do for your gums.
