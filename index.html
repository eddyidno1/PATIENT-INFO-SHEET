The orthodontic treatment journey

A structured, carefully guided path from your first visit to a confident, aligned smile — whether you're treated with fixed braces or removable clear aligners. Here's what to expect with each.

With braces

  1. 1
    Getting started

    Consultation & assessment

    Your orthodontic treatment at So Dental begins with a detailed assessment, including examination and imaging where required, to understand your alignment and bite.

  2. 2
    Planning

    Treatment planning

    A personalised treatment plan is developed, outlining the recommended approach, expected timeframe, and key stages of treatment.

  3. 3
    Fitting

    Placement of braces

    Braces are carefully placed, with adjustments made over time to guide the movement of teeth.

  4. 4
    Active treatment

    Monitoring & adjustments

    You'll visit us regularly — usually every 6–8 weeks — to check progress and adjust your braces. A typical course of orthodontic treatment takes around 18–30 months, depending on your case.

  5. 5
    Aftercare

    Retention phase

    Following treatment, retainers are commonly recommended to help maintain the alignment achieved.

With clear aligners

  1. 1
    Getting started

    Consultation & assessment

    Your clear aligner journey begins just like braces — with a thorough consultation and assessment. We examine your teeth, gums and bite, take any scans, photographs and X-rays we need, and talk through your goals to confirm that clear aligners are the right choice for you.

  2. 2
    About 2 weeks later

    Treatment plan & simulation approval

    Around two weeks after your consultation, your personalised plan is ready. Using a digital 3D scan of your teeth, we show you a simulation of how they'll move — step by step — from where they are today to the final result, and walk you through the planned movements and the expected treatment duration. Once you're happy with the plan, you approve it so your custom aligners can be manufactured.

  3. 3
    3–4 weeks later

    Start your treatment

    About three to four weeks later, your custom-made aligners are ready and treatment begins. At this visit we bond any small tooth-coloured attachments that help guide your teeth, fit your first set of aligners, and show you exactly how to insert, remove, wear and clean them — including how many hours a day to wear each set.

  4. 4
    Every 4–8 weeks

    Regular checks

    Throughout treatment you'll come in for regular progress checks, usually every four to eight weeks. We make sure your teeth are tracking to plan, hand over your next sets of aligners, and make any small refinements needed to keep everything on course.

  5. 5
    Aftercare

    Retention

    Once your teeth have reached their final position, we move into the retention phase. To keep your new smile stable and stop your teeth drifting back, we provide retainers and explain how and when to wear them for a lasting result.

Brushing with braces

Braces trap food and plaque around every bracket and wire. A careful routine keeps your teeth healthy and your treatment on schedule.

  1. 1

    Rinse first

    Swish with water to loosen food caught around brackets before you brush.

  2. 2

    Brush at an angle

    Hold a soft-bristle brush at 45° to the gumline. Brush the tops of the brackets, then angle down to clean underneath the wire. Spend a full two minutes.

  3. 3

    Clean between brackets

    Use an interdental brush (e.g. Piksters) to reach the small spaces between each bracket and the gumline where a regular brush can't fit.

  4. 4

    Floss daily with a threader

    Thread floss under the main wire with a floss threader or use super-floss, then gently clean each contact point. A water flosser is a great time-saver too.

  5. 5

    Finish with fluoride

    Rinse with a fluoride mouthwash to protect the enamel around your brackets, especially at night.

💡 Tip: Brush after every meal when you can. White marks on teeth after braces come from plaque left sitting around the brackets — a two-minute clean prevents them.

Wearing & caring for your aligners

Clear aligners only work when you wear them — and stay invisible when you look after them. Here's how to wear, clean, and store them day to day.

Wear them 22 hours a day

Wearing your aligners at least 22 hours a day is the single most important factor in effective treatment. Only take them out to eat, to drink anything other than water, and to clean your teeth.

Cold water is fine

You don't need to remove your aligners to drink cold or room-temperature water. Avoid hot drinks with them in, though — heat warps the plastic.

Rinse every time

Each time you take your aligners out, rinse them with room-temperature water to wash away saliva and plaque before storing or reinserting.

Clean twice a day

Clean your aligners twice a day with a soft toothbrush and a little mild soap. Skip toothpaste — it's abrasive and dulls the plastic.

Deep clean if discoloured

Only if they look heavily discoloured, soak them in an aligner cleaning-tablet solution for about 15 minutes (optional). An ultrasonic cleaner also works well.

Brush before reinserting

When you can, brush and floss after every meal or snack before putting your aligners back in. If you can't brush right away, at least rinse your mouth and aligners with water first.

Use your chewies

Bite on your chewies every time you put a tray in, and again for a few minutes morning and night. This seats the aligners fully against your teeth and makes treatment more effective.

Store in the case

Always keep your aligners and chewies in their case when they're out — never wrap them in a napkin, where they're easily lost or thrown away.

A little soreness is normal

It's normal for a new set to feel tight or mildly sore for a day or two as your teeth move — this is part of the process and settles quickly.

Never use hot water. Heat warps the plastic and ruins the fit. Skip coloured mouthwash, denture cleaners not made for aligners, and abrasive toothpaste — they cause staining and cloudiness.

Eating with braces

Most of your favourites are still on the menu. Just protect your brackets and wires from anything hard, sticky, or chewy.

Enjoy

  • Soft breads, pasta, rice
  • Yoghurt, cheese, smoothies
  • Steamed or soft-cooked vegetables
  • Bananas, berries, soft fruit
  • Tender meats, fish, eggs, tofu
  • Ice cream and soft desserts

Avoid

  • Hard candy, nuts, ice cubes
  • Chewing gum, caramel, toffee
  • Popcorn (kernels wreck wires)
  • Whole apples & corn on the cob
  • Crusty bagels & hard rolls
  • Chewy jerky and hard pretzels
  • Meat on the bone (ribs, chicken wings) — cut it off first

💡 Tip: You don't have to give firm foods up — cut apples, carrots, and corn off the cob into bite-size pieces and chew with your back teeth.

Handling emergencies

True orthodontic emergencies are rare. Most issues can wait until office hours — here's how to stay comfortable in the meantime.

With braces

Poking wire

Tuck a small piece of orthodontic wax over the end.

Loose or broken bracket

If it's still on the wire, leave it and cover with wax. If it comes off, save it and call us to schedule a repair.

General soreness

Normal for a few days after adjustments. Rinse with warm salt water and use your usual over-the-counter pain relief.

Lost separator

The little spacer between molars can pop out on its own near your next visit. Let us know so we can check it.

Food or object stuck

Try floss, an interdental brush, or a water flosser. Don't use anything sharp against your teeth.

Something you can't fix

When in doubt, don't force it — call the office and we'll guide you through it.

With aligners

Lost or broken aligner

Go back to your previous set to hold your progress, then call us. Don't skip ahead to the next set without checking first.

Losing track

If a gap forms between an aligner and your teeth, it's "losing track". Wear that aligner for longer each day, and use your chewies every time you reinsert it. If the gap is still there after 7 days of diligent wear, contact us.

Need help now?

Facial swelling, trauma, uncontrolled bleeding, or severe pain need prompt care — call us right away.

If an attachment or button comes off

Attachments and buttons are tiny add-ons bonded to your teeth to help your treatment work. If one comes loose it's usually not an emergency — here's what each is and what to do.

Attachment breaks

Attachments are small tooth-coloured "bumps" bonded to some teeth, giving your aligners something to grip so they can move your teeth precisely. They occasionally come off — most often in the first few days after bonding. Don't worry: keep wearing your aligners full-time as normal and contact us to arrange a quick re-bond — it's not urgent.

Button comes off

Buttons are specialised hooks bonded to your teeth for your elastics to hook onto. If a button falls off, contact us so we can have it replaced.

Wearing your elastics

Elastics (rubber bands) do the heavy lifting of aligning your bite. They only work when you wear them — consistency is everything.

How long

Wear them ~22 hours a day — all day and overnight. Only remove them to eat and to brush.

How to hook

Stretch each band between the hooks we've shown you, top to bottom. A plastic elastic-placer tool makes it easy.

Change often

Swap in a fresh pair 3 times a day, usually with each meal. Elastics lose their stretch quickly and weak ones don't move teeth.

If one snaps

Just replace it with a new one. Always carry a few spares — we'll keep you stocked at each visit.

If you forget

Don't double up to "catch up." Simply get back on track. Skipping days can add weeks to treatment.

Sore at first?

Tenderness for the first few days is normal and means they're working. It eases as your teeth adjust.
