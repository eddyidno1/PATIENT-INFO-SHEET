The orthodontic braces process

A structured, carefully guided path from your first visit to a confident, aligned smile. Here's what to expect with braces.

  1. 1
    Getting started

    Consultation & assessment

    Your orthodontic treatment at So Dental begins with a detailed assessment, including examination and imaging where required, to understand your alignment and bite.

  2. 2
    Planning

    Treatment planning

    A personalised treatment plan is developed, outlining the recommended approach, expected timeframe, and key stages of treatment.

  3. 3
    Fitting

    Placement of braces

    Braces are carefully placed, with adjustments made over time to guide the movement of teeth.

  4. 4
    Active treatment

    Monitoring & adjustments

    You'll visit us regularly — usually every 6–8 weeks — to check progress and adjust your braces. A typical course of orthodontic treatment takes around 18–30 months, depending on your case.

  5. 5
    Aftercare

    Retention phase

    Following treatment, retainers are commonly recommended to help maintain the alignment achieved.

Brushing with braces

Braces trap food and plaque around every bracket and wire. A careful routine keeps your teeth healthy and your treatment on schedule.

  1. 1

    Rinse first

    Swish with water to loosen food caught around brackets before you brush.

  2. 2

    Brush at an angle

    Hold a soft-bristle brush at 45° to the gumline. Brush the tops of the brackets, then angle down to clean underneath the wire. Spend a full two minutes.

  3. 3

    Clean between brackets

    Use an interdental brush (e.g. Piksters) to reach the small spaces between each bracket and the gumline where a regular brush can't fit.

  4. 4

    Floss daily with a threader

    Thread floss under the main wire with a floss threader or use super-floss, then gently clean each contact point. A water flosser is a great time-saver too.

  5. 5

    Finish with fluoride

    Rinse with a fluoride mouthwash to protect the enamel around your brackets, especially at night.

💡 Tip: Brush after every meal when you can. White marks on teeth after braces come from plaque left sitting around the brackets — a two-minute clean prevents them.

Eating with braces

Most of your favourites are still on the menu. Just protect your brackets and wires from anything hard, sticky, or chewy.

Enjoy

  • Soft breads, pasta, rice
  • Yoghurt, cheese, smoothies
  • Steamed or soft-cooked vegetables
  • Bananas, berries, soft fruit
  • Tender meats, fish, eggs, tofu
  • Ice cream and soft desserts

Avoid

  • Hard candy, nuts, ice cubes
  • Chewing gum, caramel, toffee
  • Popcorn (kernels wreck wires)
  • Whole apples & corn on the cob
  • Crusty bagels & hard rolls
  • Chewy jerky and hard pretzels
  • Meat on the bone (ribs, chicken wings) — cut it off first

💡 Tip: You don't have to give firm foods up — cut apples, carrots, and corn off the cob into bite-size pieces and chew with your back teeth.

Handling emergencies

True orthodontic emergencies are rare. Most issues can wait until office hours — here's how to stay comfortable in the meantime.

Poking wire

Tuck a small piece of orthodontic wax over the end.

Loose or broken bracket

If it's still on the wire, leave it and cover with wax. If it comes off, save it and call us to schedule a repair.

General soreness

Normal for a few days after adjustments. Rinse with warm salt water and use your usual over-the-counter pain relief.

Lost separator

The little spacer between molars can pop out on its own near your next visit. Let us know so we can check it.

Food or object stuck

Try floss, an interdental brush, or a water flosser. Don't use anything sharp against your teeth.

Button comes off

Buttons are specialised hooks bonded to your teeth for your elastics to hook onto. If a button falls off, contact us so we can have it replaced.

Need help now?

Facial swelling, trauma, uncontrolled bleeding, or severe pain need prompt care — call us right away.

Wearing your elastics

Elastics (rubber bands) do the heavy lifting of aligning your bite. They only work when you wear them — consistency is everything.

How long

Wear them ~22 hours a day — all day and overnight. Only remove them to eat and to brush.

How to hook

Stretch each band between the hooks we've shown you, top to bottom. A plastic elastic-placer tool makes it easy.

Change often

Swap in a fresh pair 3 times a day, usually with each meal. Elastics lose their stretch quickly and weak ones don't move teeth.

If one snaps

Just replace it with a new one. Always carry a few spares — we'll keep you stocked at each visit.

If you forget

Don't double up to "catch up." Simply get back on track. Skipping days can add weeks to treatment.

Sore at first?

Tenderness for the first few days is normal and means they're working. It eases as your teeth adjust.
